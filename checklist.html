Canton Fair & Guangzhou Sourcing Checklist

General guidance, not legal or customs advice. Check rules for your own country. 🖨️ Print or save as PDF

1. Before the trip

  • Write a one-page product spec: materials, size, colours, packaging, target retail price
  • Know your target unit cost, first-order quantity and reorder quantity
  • List required certifications and labelling for your market (e.g. CE, FCC, UKCA, product-specific rules)
  • Check visa rules and register as a buyer on the official Canton Fair site in advance
  • Pick which phase and halls match your category; plan booths to visit
  • Bring business cards, a phone with translation app, power bank, and a notebook or spreadsheet
  • Have a forwarder or rough shipping cost estimate for your destination

2. Questions at the booth

  • Are you the factory or a trading company? Where is the factory?
  • What is your main market? Do you already export to my country?
  • Can I take your catalog and price list? Are prices EXW or FOB, and which port?
  • Can you do my logo / custom packaging / colour? Extra cost and MOQ for that?
  • Which certifications and test reports can you show for this exact item?
  • Photograph the product, the booth sign and the business card together
  • Do not pay or sign on the spot; collect info and compare later

3. MOQ, lead time & payment terms

  • MOQ per item and per colour/size; is a smaller trial order possible at a higher price?
  • Sample cost, sample lead time, and whether sample cost is refunded on bulk order
  • Mass-production lead time, and how it changes around Chinese New Year
  • Payment terms (commonly a deposit plus balance before shipping); never pay 100% upfront to a new supplier
  • Pay only to a company bank account matching the contract name
  • Price validity date and currency

4. Factory or trader?

  • Check the business licence: name, registered capital, scope, registration date
  • Does the licence scope include manufacturing (生产) or only trading?
  • Ask for a live video walk-through of the production line
  • Does the address match an industrial area, not just an office building?
  • Traders are not bad; they can be useful for mixed small orders. Just know which you are dealing with

5. Sample approval

  • Order samples from 2–3 suppliers and compare side by side
  • Check size, material, finish, function, smell, packaging and labels
  • Write approval in writing with photos; keep one signed "golden sample"
  • Put all specs and the approved sample reference into the purchase order

6. Pre-shipment QC

  • Inspect when production is finished and packed, before paying the balance
  • Count quantity and check carton marks, labels and barcodes
  • Compare random units against the golden sample
  • Check workmanship, function, and measurements against spec
  • Check carton strength, packing method, and drop or shake test where relevant
  • Agree acceptable defect levels in advance (many buyers use AQL sampling)
  • Get photos/video or a written report before releasing payment

7. Shipping & Incoterms basics

  • EXW: you collect at the factory and handle everything after that
  • FOB: supplier delivers onto the vessel at the named Chinese port; you pay freight onward
  • CIF: supplier pays freight and insurance to your port; you handle import and local delivery
  • DDP: seller handles delivery including import duties; check carefully what is included
  • Choose sea, air or express based on weight, value and deadline; get quotes from your forwarder
  • Know your HS code and import duty before confirming price
  • Keep the commercial invoice, packing list and bill of lading
