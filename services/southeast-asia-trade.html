Export: China to Southeast Asia

Goods are sourced from factories across China, not from one city. Foshan is a core region for tiles. Other major production regions are in scope for the same kinds of products.

  • Ceramic and porcelain tiles and slabs
  • Sanitary ware
  • Building hardware
  • Aluminium profiles and windows
  • Light steel framing
  • Fasteners

Heavy cargo usually leaves by sea from Guangzhou's Nansha or Huangpu terminals toward ports such as Hai Phong, Ho Chi Minh City, Bangkok, Port Klang, Jakarta and Manila. Smaller or urgent lots into northern Vietnam can move by road. The construction materials export page is the product detail. This page is the lane.

Ask before you book steel. Some destinations have used trade-remedy measures on steel products, and some markets ask for a local approval before the goods can be sold. I flag that in the shortlist. Your broker confirms the current rule. This is not a legal opinion.

Import: Southeast Asia into China

The other direction is for Southeast Asian mills and quarries selling into China, and for buyers in China who want those materials sourced. I coordinate the supplier, the documents and the Guangzhou receipt. I am not a customs broker.

  • Timber, plywood and rubberwood board. Plan on plant quarantine and a certificate of origin. Wood packaging needs IPPC marks. If the species is restricted, the extra permit has to be in place before shipping. Put the species and the certificates in the contract. Vietnam, Thailand and Malaysia are the usual places to start, from a legal source.
  • Natural stone such as marble and granite. Building stone needs radiation testing. GB 6566 is the Chinese standard used for that check, and containers are often tested one by one. Indonesia and Vietnam are common origins. Nothing is released on my say-so. The lab result and your broker decide that.
  • Natural rubber. Vietnam, Cambodia and Thailand are the main origins. Any licence, inspection or duty rate has to be checked for that shipment. I do not treat an old rule as still current.
  • Rattan and bamboo. These need plant quarantine. Smaller lots are the realistic start.
  • Cement and clinker. Possible, but only after the freight and the margin are quoted. This is mostly bulk shipping, not a carton job.

I do not handle sand. That includes river sand, sea sand and construction sand. Export bans, waste rules and inspection risk make it a job I will not take.

Why Guangzhou

  • Sea. Nansha and Huangpu are the Guangzhou terminals for a full container of tile, sanitary ware or aluminium.
  • Land to Vietnam. Pingxiang (Friendship Pass) and Dongxing are the usual road gates. A planning range often quoted for those routes is about 4–10 days. The real time depends on the crossing, the queue and the cargo.
  • China–Laos railway. The line runs from Kunming through the Mohan/Boten border toward Vientiane, with a road transfer on toward Thailand when that is the destination. For inland Laos or Thailand it is often faster than sending everything by sea and then by truck.
  • Origin documents. Qualifying goods can use an ACFTA Form E (China–ASEAN) or an RCEP certificate of origin. Either one can reduce duty when the product and the paperwork meet the origin rule. That is general information, not legal or customs advice. The duty rate and whether your HS code qualifies are for your broker and the customs office at the border. I can collect the commercial documents they ask for. I do not issue the certificate.

How it works

  1. You send the direction (China to Southeast Asia, or Southeast Asia into China), the material and specs, quantity, destination and any certificate you already know you need.
  2. I research factories or suppliers and return a written shortlist with prices, MOQ and red flags.
  3. Samples ship to my Guangzhou address. Photo and video QC with a written note starts at $60. See the sample consolidation packs ($60 / $120 / $180).
  4. Bulk lots consolidate in Guangzhou. Coordination starts at $80, plus a transport quote. See GZ consolidation.
  5. I hand the cargo and the documents to your forwarder. Export terms are EXW at the factory or FOB at a major Chinese port: Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Ningbo, Shanghai or Qingdao. For imports, the same Guangzhou address can receive the samples and I coordinate papers with your forwarder. Full DDP is not offered.
