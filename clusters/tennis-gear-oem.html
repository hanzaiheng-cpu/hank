Custom tennis gear sourcing (OEM / private label)

Want your own brand of tennis balls, grips, strings or bags? I find Chinese suppliers and check what can actually be verified before you order.

What can be sourced

  • Tennis balls (practice, training, custom-printed)
  • Overgrips and replacement grips
  • Strings (polyester, synthetic gut, multifilament)
  • Racket bags and backpacks
  • Apparel and accessories: wristbands, headbands, caps, socks, dampeners
  • Rackets: possible, but fewer suppliers and longer development, discussed case by case

What you can verify, and how I check it

  • Business licence: I check the supplier on China's official company registry and confirm it matches the company you pay.
  • Factory audit: I visit (or video-call from the floor) to confirm it is a real factory making your product, not only a trader.
  • Certificates (ISO 9001, BSCI and similar): I ask for the certificate and check it with the issuing body's public lookup where one exists, and that the company name and address match.
  • Lab test reports (SGS, Intertek, Bureau Veritas and similar): I ask for the report and check product, date and report number with the lab where it offers verification.
  • Material tests: where your market requires them, such as EU REACH or US CPSIA for children's products, I ask for recent reports or arrange testing through a lab you choose.

Match balls and ITF approval

The ITF states that any ball used in a tournament played under the Rules of Tennis must be named on its list of approved balls. The current list and approval procedures are published here: ITF: List of Approved Tennis Balls. To sell a ball as approved, that exact model must be on the list, so I check the list against any supplier claim. Practice and training balls don't need approval.

I never claim a factory holds a certificate until I have seen and checked the document for your order.
